The Nokia N900 (RX-51) came out in late 2009. It is a pocket computer that happens to be a phone: a 3.5 inch touch screen, a **slide-out QWERTY keyboard**, and a real Linux system,
**Maemo 5 "Fremantle"**, with a desktop of its own, the usual GNU tools and a terminal. You can open it up, install a compiler on it and run a web server. That is why people still use it
more than fifteen years later, and why this project exists.

{% include figure.html src="n900-01.webp" alt="The N900 with its stylus lying on the screen" caption="The N900 with the stylus. The screen is resistive: a fingernail or the stylus works as well as a fingertip." %}

## What it is good for today

- A pocket **Linux terminal** with a hardware keyboard (ssh, editors, git, tmux, mutt, weechat, see the [packages]({{ '/packages/' | relative_url }})).
- Radio, podcasts and RSS reading, a calendar and notes, with software you can build and repair yourself.
- Learning and tinkering: the whole system is open, documented by a community, and the hardware is easy to get second-hand.

It is **not** a modern smartphone: the web browser is old, mobile networks without 2G/3G are out of reach and apps from app stores do not exist. The [specifications](../specifications/) show the limits.
